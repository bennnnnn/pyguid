## Why this matters

Many languages use `{ }` for blocks. Python uses **indentation**. A short preview now prevents shock when you meet **`if`** and **`for`**.

## Indented line = belongs to the line above

After a line ending with **`:`**, the next lines are **indented** (usually **4 spaces**) to show they are **inside** that block:

<PythonExample
  filename="indent.py"
  code={`if True:
    print("This is indented")
print("This is not")`}
/>

**Output**

```text
This is indented
This is not
```

- Line 2 is **inside** the `if` (extra spaces)
- Line 3 is **outside** — runs every time

## Do not teach `if` deeply yet

You do not need to master conditions here. Notice only:

1. Line ends with **`:`**
2. Next line(s) are **indented**
3. Back to the **left edge** = block ended

Full **`if`** lessons come in **Conditions**.

## Same idea later

| Feature | Uses indentation |
| ------- | ---------------- |
| `if` / `elif` / `else` | yes |
| `for` / `while` | yes |
| `def` functions | yes |
| `class` | yes |

## Common preview mistake

<PythonExample
  filename="bad_indent.py"
  code={`if True:
print("Oops")`}
/>

Mixed tabs/spaces or missing indent → **IndentationError**. Align the `print` under the `if` with spaces.

<Callout variant="tip">
  Use **4 spaces** per indent level. Avoid the Tab key until your editor is configured for spaces.
</Callout>

## Recap

- **`:`** starts a block; indented lines belong to it
- **Left edge** = top level, not inside a block
- Deep **`if`** / loop rules come in later chapters
