## Why this matters

The **`=`** sign looks like math class, but in Python it means something different. Mixing up **assignment** and **equality** is a common beginner trap—clear now saves confusion later.

## `=` means “store in this name”

In Python, **`=`** does **not** mean “equal” the way it does in algebra.

**`=`** means: **store this value in this name.**

<PythonExample filename="assign.py" code={`score = 10`} />

Read it: **put the value 10 into the name `score`.**

The **name** goes on the **left**. The **value** goes on the **right**.

## Wrong way around

<Callout variant="danger" title="Invalid assignment">
  **`10 = score`** is **not** allowed. Python needs a **name** on the left to label the storage.
</Callout>

## Assignment vs comparison (preview)

You will use **`==`** later to **compare** two values. For now, notice the difference:

```python
score = 10      # assignment — store 10 in score
score == 10     # comparison — asks “is score equal to 10?” (later)
```

<PythonExample
  filename="store.py"
  code={`score = 10
print(score)`}
/>

Output: **`10`** — you stored it, then printed what lives under **`score`**.

## Recap

- **`=`** = **assignment** (“put this value in this name”)
- **Left** = variable name, **right** = value
- **`==`** is for **comparison**, not storing—covered when you learn conditions
