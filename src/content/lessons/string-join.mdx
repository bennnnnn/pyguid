**`join()`** is called on the **separator** string. It joins a list of strings into one string.

<PythonExample
  filename="join.py"
  code={`words = ["Python", "is", "great"]
sentence = " ".join(words)
print(sentence)`}
/>

## Different separators

<PythonExample
  filename="separators.py"
  code={`items = ["2024", "05", "23"]
print("-".join(items))
print(", ".join(["a", "b", "c"]))`}
/>

## join() after split()

<PythonExample
  filename="roundtrip.py"
  code={`original = "one,two,three"
parts = original.split(",")
restored = ",".join(parts)
print(restored)`}
/>

## All items must be strings

<PythonExample
  filename="str_first.py"
  code={`nums = [1, 2, 3]
text = "-".join(str(n) for n in nums)
print(text)`}
/>

<Callout variant="danger">
  `"-".join([1, 2, 3])` raises **`TypeError`** — convert numbers with `str()` first.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="tip">
  `join()` is often faster and clearer than many `+` operations in a loop.
</Callout>
