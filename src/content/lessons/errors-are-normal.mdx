## Why this matters

Beginners sometimes read **SyntaxError** or **NameError** as judgment. Professionals read them as **rough GPS** — “you are close; adjust one detail.” Changing that mindset cuts frustration in half.

## Errors are conversations, not grades

Python does **not** know you personally. When it raises an error, it is saying:

- “I stopped here.”
- “This is the rule I could not satisfy.”

<PythonExample filename="tiny_success.py" code={`print("I run successfully")`} />

That runs cleanly. Breaking one character flips Python into “tell me what is wrong” mode — useful, not embarrassing.

## The habit that wins

Expect errors **many times per session**. Change **one clue**, click **Run** again — that loop is literally how shipped software is made.

<Callout variant="tip">
  If feelings spike, breathe and read the **last line** of the error first—the next lesson series builds that reflex step by step.
</Callout>

## Recap

- **Everyone** triggers errors — often on purpose while debugging
- Errors point to **grammar or names** early on; fixing them teaches **patterns**
- Treat red text as **signal**, not **verdict**
